# DASS Proof-of-Concept Brief

**For academic supervision and early-stage sponsor discussion**

## Project at a glance

DASS is a research-stage modular electromechanical device-management and training platform intended exclusively for dedicated inert, non-functional training props. The proposed proof of concept focuses on a serviceable electronics enclosure, local device-health monitoring, session records and a desktop management demonstration.

DASS is not proposed as an attachment for functional firearms. The project excludes live ammunition, firearm operating mechanisms, aiming or targeting assistance, ballistic calculations and recoil replication.

## Problem hypothesis

Some training environments may benefit from portable, serviceable devices whose availability, basic health status and maintenance history can be reviewed locally. This need must be confirmed with representative training staff and technicians before it is treated as a validated customer requirement.

## Proposed proof of concept

The first demonstrator is deliberately bounded:

1. One dedicated inert, non-functional training prop as the physical host.
2. A removable, serviceable electronics enclosure.
3. Basic power and device-health monitoring.
4. A local software demonstrator for device status, session metadata, synthetic scenario analysis and CSV reports; a graphical desktop application remains a later development milestone.
5. A documented test plan and costed bill of materials.

The initial demonstrator will not include multi-site deployment, cloud services or a production-ready product.

## Success criteria to agree with the supervisor

The numerical acceptance thresholds should be established after selecting components and defining the laboratory environment. Candidate criteria include:

- All required status fields are recorded and exported correctly.
- The software operates locally without mandatory internet access.
- A fixed software seed produces repeatable synthetic demonstration records.
- The enclosure allows documented access to designated serviceable modules.
- Bench measurements are traceable to instruments and test conditions.
- The BOM identifies supplier, part number, quantity, currency and quotation date.
- No unverified estimate is reported as a measured performance result.

## Proposed six-month schedule

The detailed schedule is maintained in [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md). The sequence is requirements and literature review, concept/CAD, software and electronics demonstrator, inert-host enclosure prototype, verification and cost analysis, then final reporting.

## Support requested for a first-stage proposal

The first-stage request should be limited to resources needed for a proof of concept:

- Academic supervision and design reviews.
- Access to approved CAD and electronics laboratory facilities.
- A small, documented prototype-parts budget.
- Access to appropriate measurement equipment.
- Feedback from representative training staff and maintenance personnel through an approved process.

The budget should be submitted with a supplier-quoted BOM and a clear contingency. No unsupported total should be stated before the design and component choices are fixed.

## Exclusions and limitations

This brief does not claim that DASS is production-ready, independently validated, certified or endorsed by a military organization. No physical prototype or measured hardware performance is claimed at this stage. Any physical demonstrator is limited to the approved inert, non-functional training-prop environment.
