# DASS Product Positioning and Differentiation Hypotheses

## 1. Product statement

DASS is a proposed modular electromechanical training-device and device-management platform for dedicated inert, non-functional training props. The product concept combines a serviceable electronics enclosure, device-health monitoring, local session software and a future multi-device administration layer.

DASS is not positioned as a new general-purpose shooting simulator. Existing training simulators and sensor accessories already occupy that broad category. The project must identify and evidence a narrower engineering contribution.

## 2. Intended customer problems to validate

These are hypotheses to investigate with instructors, technicians and procurement stakeholders; they are not claims about all training organizations.

- Small training sites may need a portable and simpler-to-maintain device-management option.
- Technicians may value replaceable electronic subassemblies and clear diagnostics.
- Training staff may benefit from local session records and device availability information.
- Buyers may require transparent lifecycle cost, spare-part availability and documented support arrangements.
- Some organizations may prefer local operation and limited dependence on cloud services.

## 3. Differentiation hypotheses

| Hypothesis | Potential value | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|
| Serviceable modular electronics | Reduced repair time and module-level replacement | Timed serviceability study and failure/replacement records |
| Local-first software | Operation without mandatory cloud connectivity | Offline integration test |
| Device-health and maintenance records | Better visibility of availability and service history | Usability study with training staff and technicians |
| Open, documented interfaces | Easier academic review and controlled integration | Interface documentation and software tests |
| Local component sourcing | Potential supply-chain and cost resilience | Supplier quotations, lead-time comparison and second-source analysis |
| Transparent lifecycle costing | More credible procurement assessment | Costed BOM, maintenance assumptions and sensitivity analysis |

No cost, reliability, portability or performance advantage should be claimed until the relevant evidence exists.

## 5. Stakeholder discovery questions

Before fixing the final requirements, interview or consult representative stakeholders through the university's approved process:

### Training staff
- What information is needed before, during and after a training session?
- Which device-status problems cause the most disruption?
- Is local operation important in the intended environment?

### Technicians
- Which components are most difficult to inspect or replace?
- What diagnostic information would reduce troubleshooting time?
- What spare parts and service records are currently maintained?

### Procurement and management
- Which lifecycle-cost categories matter?
- What documentation is needed for a pilot evaluation?
- What requirements apply to safety, data handling, support and local sourcing?

Record anonymized findings and obtain any required institutional approval before collecting user data.

## 6. What DASS should demonstrate to a potential sponsor

A credible early-stage proposal should contain:
1. A clearly bounded user need supported by stakeholder evidence.
2. A requirements document and architecture.
3. A working software demonstrator using clearly labelled synthetic data.
4. A CAD concept and a safe prototype intended only for an inert, non-functional host.
5. A costed bill of materials based on dated quotations.
6. A verification plan with measurable acceptance criteria.
7. A risk register and honest statement of uncompleted work.

The first funding request should support a bounded proof of concept, not imply that a production-ready system already exists.

## 7. Positioning statement

DASS aims to differentiate through serviceability, local-first device management, transparent lifecycle costing and a documented, modular architecture for inert training equipment. Each proposed advantage remains a hypothesis until tested against comparable systems and stakeholder needs.
